The lead answer

“Our module turns a raw Sentinel-1 radar scene into court-ready slick geometry — polygon, centre, area, confidence — in 10 seconds on a CPU. On validation that deliberately includes look-alike traps, it finds two-thirds of environmentally significant slicks with ~85 m centre accuracy — and when it draws a slick, it’s right nearly 1 time in 2, versus 1 in 22 for the best classical method. On our demo scene it measured a 268 km² slick to within half a percent.”

Layer 2 · intuition

Why the classical method loses

“Oil looks dark in radar, so the obvious approach is flag everything dark — we actually built and measured it: it scores Dice 0.06, because the ocean is full of dark things that aren’t oil — calm wind, algae, rain cells. Our network learned the difference from 2,500 real scenes, including 685 deliberate trap scenes. This problem isn’t finding dark spots — it’s rejecting the fakes.”
Layer 3 · how we improved it · your best material

Diagnosis, not guesswork

“Object-level error analysis showed our false positives were confidently wrong — look-alike regions predicted as oil at 80% confidence, which ordinary cross-entropy barely penalizes once averaged over millions of pixels. So we switched to Focal loss, which amplifies the gradient on exactly those pixels — and object precision jumped 66% in one fine-tune (0.28 → 0.47) while slick recall stayed within three points. The diagnosis, the failed alternatives, and the winning run are all in our git history.”
Layer 4 · ML-literate judge

Why our numbers are conservative

“Three deliberate choices. Scene-level splits — no leakage. Look-alike hard negatives inside validation — scored the way most papers score, oil-only, our model reaches ~0.75; we report the harder 0.35 because it predicts deployed behaviour. A sealed test set — evaluated exactly once, after the design freezes. Every operating parameter was selected on validation only.”

The trade · if recall vs precision comes up

Own it explicitly

“We chose to double alarm trustworthiness at a three-point recall cost — for an investigation tool, a false accusation is costlier than a delayed detection.”
The limitation · volunteer it first

Don’t wait to be caught

“One error class remains: a minority of very dark, structured look-alikes still fool the model with high confidence. In our 10-scene demo panel, 9 scenes are clean or correct; one look-alike scene retains false positives — we show that scene ourselves, and our roadmap targets it with curriculum training.”
Never say

Lines that lose the room

  • “Our model is 95% accurate.” — pixel accuracy on 96%-background imagery is meaningless.
  • “Better than paper X’s number.” — different datasets and regimes are never head-to-head comparable.
  • “0.35 Dice” with no baseline. — it always travels with 0.06 and the reason (traps included).
  • “91% chance it’s oil.” — scores are uncalibrated; say “detection confidence”.
The closer

Evidence, not claims

“Everything is reproducible — versioned splits, per-experiment manifests, failed experiments in the git log next to the successful ones. And the number that matters most doesn’t exist yet: the test set is sealed, evaluated exactly once when the design freezes. That’s what makes our final number one you can trust.”
