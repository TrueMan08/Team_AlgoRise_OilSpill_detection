Layer 1 · any judge · 30 seconds

The lead answer

“Our current operational model combines X1c segmentation, C3 scene context, and a V1 component verifier. On a held-out validation split that includes look-alike traps, it reaches 81.7% object precision and 81.2% recall for slicks of at least 10 ha. That is a 34.3 percentage-point precision gain over the E5 MVP baseline evaluated on the same split. Our X2 challenger reaches 0.736 pixel Dice. The Part III test set remains sealed, and hosted deployment and full-scene timing are pending.”

Layer 2 · intuition

Why the classical method loses

“Oil looks dark in radar, so the obvious approach is flag everything dark — we actually built and measured it: it scores Dice 0.06, because the ocean is full of dark things that aren’t oil — calm wind, algae, rain cells. Our network learned the difference from 2,500 real scenes, including 685 deliberate trap scenes. This problem isn’t finding dark spots — it’s rejecting the fakes.”
Layer 3 · how we improved it · your best material

X2 is a separate pixel-Dice challenger, not the selected operational pipeline.”

Counter-attack · “papers report 90%+”

Turn it into a win

“Published figures often use different datasets and evaluation protocols, so we avoid claiming direct superiority over them. Our own comparisons use the same held-out validation split and disclose the thresholds and matching procedure.”
The trade · if recall vs precision comes up

Own it explicitly

“The recall-focused setting reaches 84.8% large-slick recall at 67.6% precision. The operational setting raises precision to 81.7% with 81.2% large-slick recall. Teams can choose the tradeoff for their review workflow.”
The limitation · volunteer it first

Don’t wait to be caught

“These are validation results, not a sealed-test result. Some structured look-alikes still produce false positives. The service package is prepared, but hosted deployment and full-scene CPU timing have not yet been established.”
Never say

Lines that lose the room

  • “Our model is 95% accurate.” — pixel accuracy on 96%-background imagery is meaningless.
  • “Better than paper X’s number.” — different datasets and regimes are never head-to-head comparable.
  • “Our test performance is 81.7% / 81.2%.” — these are validation metrics; the Part III test set remains sealed.
  • “91% chance it’s oil.” — scores are uncalibrated; say “detection confidence”.
The closer

Evidence, not claims

“Everything is reproducible — versioned splits, per-experiment manifests, failed experiments in the git log next to the successful ones. And the number that matters most doesn’t exist yet: the test set is sealed, evaluated exactly once when the design freezes. That’s what makes our final number one you can trust.”
